# Abstract

**Background:** Automated epileptic seizure detection from continuous scalp electroencephalography (EEG) is crucial for timely clinical intervention. **Problem:** Conventional models frequently overfit patient morphology, generate excessive false alarms under severe class imbalance (~344:1), or fail under patient-independent evaluation. **Method:** We present NeuroAegis, an architecture integrating a channel-preserving 1D-CNN, a frozen spatial graph convolutional network (GNN, $\theta=0.30$, 23 nodes, 40 edges), and a causal unidirectional gated recurrent unit (GRU, sequence length $L=8$, 22.5 s context) totaling 91,858 parameters. **Dataset & Evaluation:** Evaluated on a strictly held-out CHB-MIT test cohort (4 patients, 155 continuous records, 152.82 hours, 219,909 5.0-second windows with 50% overlap) at frozen decision threshold $\tau=0.50$. **Results:** The system achieved 95.45% event sensitivity (21/22 seizures detected), 0.98970 AUROC, 0.80681 AUPRC, 0.68025 F1 score, and 62.66 false alarms/24h (-96.8% reduction versus 1D-CNN baseline), with a median detection delay of 9.0 seconds. **XAI:** Integrated Gradients revealed salient attributions in temporal-parietal channels (`T7-P7`, `P3-O1`), validated through monotonic perturbation deletion curves ($0.8093 \to 0.2011$). **Cross-Domain Evaluation:** Zero-shot transfer on an external Siena Scalp EEG benchmark subset (2 patients, 4 seizures, 2.46 hours) achieved 100% event sensitivity and 0.0 FA/24h. Post-hoc calibration on held-out patient PN12 improved F1 from 0.3043 to 0.3750 (+23.2%). **Limitations & Contribution:** This retrospective study demonstrates that spatial filtering suppresses false alarms while causal temporal modeling restores seizure sensitivity. Clinician validation was not performed, and prospective multi-center trials remain future work.
